01

Everything in one file

Slides, script, audio and a book go into one HTML file (.ega.html). Open it in a browser and it plays; open it in the editor and you can change it. Send it by email, put it on your school's server, or upload it to an LMS (SCORM): it is the same single file every time.

02

Show it in the order you speak

Figures and equations appear one at a time, in the order you explain them (steps). Add a script and you get spoken audio and captions, and you can export the lesson as a video.

03

An AI drafts; the quality check finds what to fix

Tell a chat AI your topic and it drafts the lesson. The quality check looks for text that overflows, text that is too small, color contrast and the alt text of images, and even writes the fix request for you.

04

Learners read it again, on their own device

Learners can go back over a lesson at their own pace, on a phone or tablet. They can read it as a book, highlight passages, and hide the highlighted words to test their recall. Highlights stay on that device only; they are not saved in the file or sent anywhere.

05

Education, business, everyday life

The samples page is in Japanese; some of the samples are in English.

  • Education Classes, lectures, staff training
  • Business Reports, proposals, company training
  • Everyday life Guides from local government, health care and disaster preparedness

06

An open format

Egatari is a file format that is not tied to any one app. Anyone can read the specification (CC BY 4.0), and lessons can be made with any tool.

07

Get started

  1. The editor (nothing to install)

    Open studio.egatari.org in Chrome or Edge and make your lesson right there.

  2. With a chat AI

    Install the skill for Claude or Codex and ask it for a lesson.

  3. Command line

    npx egatari new mytalk --lang en
npx egatari dev mytalk/deck.html
